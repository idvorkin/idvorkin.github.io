I've spent most of my life building an impressive ego. Senior engineer at Meta. Dad of two. The guy who bikes everywhere and does magic tricks at parties. These aren't lies - they're real parts of who I am. But here's what I'm learning: when I'm living entirely for that résumé, when I'm performing my identity instead of living it, something feels hollow.

{% include ai-slop.html percent="90" %}

Carl Jung had a framework for this tension that I keep coming back to. He distinguished between the Ego (the social self we present to the world) and the Self (the totality of who we are, including the parts we hide or don't know yet). The relationship between these two determines whether we're living authentically or just playing a role.

Much of what I'm exploring here comes from {% include amazon.html asin="087773576X" title="Ego and Archetype by Edward F. Edinger" %}, which is the clearest map I've found for understanding this dynamic. Edinger takes Jung's dense concepts and makes them practical.

This is still a draft: the framework is here, my own experience mostly isn't yet.

## What Ego and Self Actually Mean

**Ego** is the conscious "I" that deals with the world: your roles (parent, engineer, friend), your achievements, your reputation, the story you tell about yourself. You need it to hold a job and keep relationships. The trap is that it runs on approval, so when you over-identify with it, your worth rides on the résumé.

**Self** is the whole of you: the parts you show, the parts you hide, the anger and grief you avoid, and the potential you haven't used yet. Jung saw it as the organizing center of the psyche, the part that says "this isn't right" about a prestigious job, or pulls you toward work that doesn't pay.

## The Four-Stage Cycle

The Ego-Self relationship isn't static. You go around this cycle repeatedly, at deeper levels each time.

1. **Ego inflation: "I am my achievements."** Worth is tied to output, comparison is constant, and you're exhausted but can't slow down. Society rewards this, so it's easy to stay here. My version is [treating life like a business](/life-as-business): systematize and measure your way to worth. It worked, until the more I achieved, the more I needed to achieve to feel okay.
2. **Ego deflation: the story breaks.** Burnout, failure, loss, or a creeping "this can't be all there is." Success feels hollow and roles feel like a cage. It's necessary: the parts you ignored while building the impressive life are demanding attention.
3. **Dialogue with Self.** Turning inward and meeting the shadow: the suppressed anger, the avoided grief, the desires you ruled "unacceptable." For me this looks like [meditation](/siy) where I sit with what I feel instead of problem-solving, and [time off](/time-off) where I step out of achievement mode on purpose.
4. **Integration: Ego in service of Self.** You keep the roles and keep achieving, but you're not trapped by them. Praise and criticism become data. It isn't a destination; you cycle back through, and it's a spiral, not a line.

## Practices for the Shift

- **Solitude**: time alone with no agenda; walks without podcasts.
- **Creating with no audience**: writing that never gets published, magic practice just for the joy of it.
- **Depth work**: [coaching](/coaching) or therapy that goes past problem-solving; journaling without editing.
- **Shadow work**: notice what triggers you in others (often a disowned part of yourself), and ask what parts of you you've exiled to keep this identity.

## The Paradox: Ego Isn't the Enemy

The goal isn't to destroy the Ego or transcend it. It's to right-size it: Self leads, Ego executes. The problem is forgetting that the role is something you're playing, not who you are.

My [Tesla vs bike identity crisis](/bike-tesla-identity) is the small version. My Ego wanted a simple story: "I'm a car-free person" or "I'm a Tesla driver." The Self knows it's both. The Ego wants a consistent story; the Self is comfortable with complexity.

I still catch myself in Ego inflation, chasing metrics and needing validation. The difference is I notice it faster, and can ask: "Is this serving my Ego or my Self?"
