I parked pistol squats in September. I was doing box pistols to a stack of plates, and I decided Bulgarian split squats were enough for the leg day. Now a cranky glute med has me thinking about what it would take to come back, and to finally get a real one, floor to standing. This is the plan I'm keeping for when I say go, built on Michael Perry's StrongFirst progression.

## Why pistols, when I have Bulgarians?

Bulgarians are the builder. Pistols are the test. Bulgarians load the leg, and I can add 5 lb at a time. A pistol asks whether I control that leg through its full range on one foot, with nothing else touching the floor.

|             | Bulgarian split squat                  | Pistol                                                      |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| My number   | 50 lb/hand, 3×8/side                   | Box pistols to a 4-plate stack, then parked                 |
| Builds      | Quad and glute strength under load     | Full-depth knee and hip bend, ankle range, one-foot balance |
| Stability   | Two contact points (front foot, bench) | One foot. The glute med and the foot do the steadying       |
| Spine risk  | Arching                                | Rounding at the bottom                                      |
| Equipment   | Dumbbells and a bench                  | Floor and one 8–12 kg bell. Works at home                   |
| Progress by | Weight                                 | Depth (lower the box), then less counterweight              |

Perry sells them as stronger legs, better lower-body flexibility, and fewer tweaks. For me the honest extra is ankle and hip range plus balance, which Bulgarians barely touch.

## The ladder

Perry splits the progression into two parts. Part 1 earns the range: hips and ankles that let me sit all the way down with heels on the floor. Part 2 builds the pistol in pieces: the bottom, then the top, then the way down, then the way up. A light bell (he says 8–12 kg) rides along the whole way as a counterweight. His warning up front: "[This isn't an exercise most people can jump right into.](https://www.strongfirst.com/how-to-achieve-your-first-pistol-squat/#:~:text=This%20isn%E2%80%99t%20an%20exercise%20most%20people%20can%20jump%20right%20into)"

The article mostly says "once you own it" before moving on. I don't trust myself with that, so each step below gets a "ready when" test I can pass or fail. Those tests are mine, not his.

### Part 1: Earn the range

1. **Prying goblet squat.** Bell at the chest, sit deep, elbows inside the knees. Press the knees out and rock side to side. Then shift onto one foot and drive that knee forward over mid-foot, heel down, to work the ankle. 30–45 s per side, 2–3 rounds.
   - _Ready when:_ 45 s per side in the bottom, heels flat, no pinch at the front of the hip.
2. **Weight shift with the bell.** Still in the bottom, rock toward the toes and back toward the heels with the whole foot down. Feel the lever: bell out, chest up. Bell in, I fold and round.
   - _Ready when:_ 5 slow rocks each way, feet flat, and I can feel the bell steering my torso.
3. **Feet-together deep squat.** Same squat, knees together, elbows outside them. Rock forward with the bell at shin height, back with it at the chest.
   - _Ready when:_ a 30 s hold with feet touching and heels down, plus 5 rocks each way without tipping onto my butt.

### Part 2: Build the pistol in pieces

<!-- prettier-ignore-start -->

{: start="4"}
4. **Slide one leg out at the bottom.** From the feet-together squat, rock back and raise the bell to shoulder height in the same moment. That frees a foot. Grip hard, sniff in a breath, brace, and slide the leg straight out. Alternate sides. Perry promises a few falls on the butt along the way.
   - _Ready when:_ 3 clean slides per side, no falls, two sessions in a row. That's owning the bottom.
5. **The "small" position, from the top.** Standing, elbows glued to the sides, bell tight to the belly. Hinge deep and curl up small: knee over mid-foot, heel down, spine still. Two feet first, then one. Don't hold it long on one leg; it burns energy I need for the last two steps.
   - _Ready when:_ I reach it on one leg 3 times per side, heel down, spine still.
6. **The descent.** From small on one leg, push the bell away and shoot the free leg forward together. Perry's cue: "[The rate at which you push the kettlebell forward is the same rate in which you shoot your leg through.](https://www.strongfirst.com/how-to-achieve-your-first-pistol-squat/#:~:text=The%20rate%20at%20which%20you%20push%20the%20kettlebell%20forward%20is%20the%20same%20rate%20in%20which%20you%20shoot%20your%20leg%20through)" At the bottom, park the bell wherever keeps me from tipping back.
   - _Ready when:_ 3 lowerings per side, about 3 s each, to floor depth, no crash.
7. **Stand up.** Squeeze the bell, sniff in more air, grunt to start, and hold the brace all the way up. Perry: "[Repeat as needed but keep the volume low.](https://www.strongfirst.com/how-to-achieve-your-first-pistol-squat/#:~:text=Repeat%20as%20needed%20but%20keep%20the%20volume%20low)"
   - _Done when:_ one rep, floor to standing, heel down, spine still. First pistol.

<!-- prettier-ignore-end -->

After the first one, Perry programs them 2–3 days a week at very low volume: a few ladders of 3-2-1 per side, 5–8 sets of 2, or 8–10 singles.

## Where I am

Over the summer I was doing pistols to a stack of four 10 lb plates with a kettlebell on top, and they were getting more stable. The scale was stack height: pull a plate, and it gets harder. My rule was that a plate comes off after 3 clean reps from the box. Then I parked them, and the single-leg day became Bulgarians only. I also decided pistols don't get tacked on after Bulgarians. If they come back, they get their own slot.

Looking at that against Perry's ladder, I was working steps 6 and 7, the descent and the stand-up, cut short by a box. That's a legitimate lane. It builds the top half. Two things were missing:

- **The bottom.** I never tested steps 1–4. The last few inches are a mobility problem, not a strength problem, and a box hides that.
- **The counterweight.** My kettlebell was there for height, not in my hands. Perry's bell is held out front, and it's what keeps you from tipping back at depth. Adding it is a new variable, so I expect it to change how the old box height feels.

One more fix: measure the box in centimeters. "Four plates" depends on which plates I grab. A height doesn't.

## The re-entry plan

### The gate

No pistols until my glute med passes all three of these, 7 days running:

- No lateral hip pain walking, on stairs, or lying on that side.
- 30 s on one leg on the sore side, pelvis level, no pain.
- McGill side plank on both sides, no pain.

The glute med is what stops the knee caving and the hip dropping on one leg, and a pistol loads it more than anything else I do. Bulgarians wait on the same gate.

### Test day

1. Steps 1–3 on the floor with a light bell, about 5 minutes. Note which tests I pass.
2. Step 4: 3 slides per side. Pass or fail, both are data. This tells me where the bottom is.
3. Box pistols one plate higher than where I left off, bell in hand, 2×2 per side, 3 s down.
4. Stop on any lateral hip pain, knee cave, or low-back rounding. Check the hip the next morning. A clean morning unlocks week 2.

### The 4-week ramp

The single-leg slot alternates: pistols one week, Bulgarians the next, and nothing stacked after Bulgarians. Frequency comes from a 4-minute floor drill in the warm-up on kettlebell days. It's bodyweight plus a light bell, so it costs almost nothing.

| Week | Single-leg slot                                                                       | Warm-up drill | Move on when                |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| 1    | Pistols: test day. 5-plate box + bell, 2×2/side                                       | Steps 1–3     | Clean hip next morning      |
| 2    | Bulgarians, exhaling on every rep up                                                  | Steps 1–4     | Bulgarians clean, hip quiet |
| 3    | Pistols. 4-plate box + bell, 2 ladders of 3-2-1/side, then 2 floor lowerings per side | Steps 1–4     | Every rep clean             |
| 4    | Bulgarians                                                                            | Steps 1–5     | Steps 4 and 5 passed        |

After that, every clean pistol session takes a plate off, until the box is gone and step 7 is one rep from the floor. At one pistol session every two weeks, that's months. It's slower than Perry's 2–3 days a week, on purpose: it protects the Bulgarian progression and the hip.

Stop signs, every session: lateral hip pain, knee drifting inside the big toe, low back rounding at the bottom, or lightheadedness. Any one ends pistol work for the day, and the next session goes back one plate higher.

## Faults and fixes

- **Heel lifts at the bottom.** The ankle is out of range. More step-1 prying, and stay at the box height where the heel stays down. Don't chase depth on my toes.
- **Tipping backward.** My center of mass is behind my foot. Push the bell farther out and practice the step-2 rocks. That's the counterweight's whole job, and it's why my old box pistols felt tippy.
- **Knee caves in.** The glute med isn't holding the line. Raise the box until the knee tracks over mid-foot. A cave is a stop sign, not something to push through.
- **Low back rounds.** Back safety comes before depth. Brace the way Perry teaches it, and set the box at the lowest height where my spine stays still. Only go lower once that's clean.
- **Free leg drops or cramps.** Holding a straight leg out front is a hip flexor job. Stand on a low step so the free heel can hang below the standing foot. Don't fight a cramp.
- **Crashing into the bottom.** Bouncing out of a crash isn't control. 3 s down on every rep. If I can't control the last inches, the box is too low.
- **Holding my breath.** Perry's brace is a short hold for one rep, and I've gotten lightheaded on Bulgarians from holding too long. Keep sets to 1–3 reps, exhale at the top, full breath before the next.
- **Too much, too soon.** Pistols are a skill, and grinding reps teaches the grind. Stop the set while the reps still look identical.
