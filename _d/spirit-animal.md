## Hands that see

Nearly two-thirds of the raccoon's sensory cortex handles touch, a bigger share than in any other animal studied ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Senses)). A raccoon understands the world through its hands.

So do I. The things I love most are hand things: cards that change color on command, coins passing through a spectator's hand, [balloons](/balloon), and back in my 30s, juggling fire. My [Dealer of Smiles and Wonder](/eulogy#dealer-of-smiles-and-wonder) role is basically a raccoon with a deck of cards.

## The lock-picker with a long memory

In a 1908 study, raccoons opened 11 of 13 complex locks in fewer than 10 tries, and later studies show they remember the solutions for at least three years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Intelligence)).

That's my [Technologist](/eulogy#technologist) role: vim, tmux, a dotfile collection, a vim plugin for OneNote, and more [pet projects](/pet-projects) than I'll admit to. Puzzles that would annoy other people are the fun part for me.

The other half of lock-picking is trial and error: try, fail, try again. There's no great study of raccoon "persistence" as its own trait, so I'll keep this claim small. But it's the shape of my discipline too. I start strong, fall off the wagon, and [restart the next day](/affirmations#do-it-anyways-deliberate-disciplined-daily) without beating myself up. Grandmother mind, for a raccoon.

Raccoons are also tinkerers. In Laramie, about one in four wild raccoons solved a new puzzle box, mostly the young ones, each in its own way ([University of Wyoming, 2024](https://www.uwyo.edu/news/2024/07/laramie-raccoons-adapt-to-new-foraging-challenges.html)). In a 2017 version of Aesop's fable test, where the trick is to drop stones in a tube to raise the water, one raccoon skipped the stones and tipped the whole apparatus over ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/news/2017/10/animals-intelligence-raccoons-birds-aesops/)). I relate. My [AI journal](/ai-journal#ai-helps-me-think-by-showing-me-options) is mostly me trying everything, having AI show me a field of options, and picking by eye.

## Wet paws first

Raccoons don't wash their food. That's a myth. Wetting the paws makes their touch nerves more responsive ([Rasmusson & Turnbull, 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3797915/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Dousing)). Why they douse in the wild is still debated, but the effect is clear: the raccoon sharpens its senses before it handles anything.

I do the same thing every morning. 5am, a [gratitude journal](/process-journal), then 20 minutes of meditation. It's not cleaning up the day. It's getting my senses ready to handle it. That's the [Emotionally Healthy Human](/eulogy#emotionally-healthy-human) role, and it's the raccoon fact I like most.

## The mask and the mischief

The dark mask may cut glare and help night vision. That's a hypothesis, not settled science ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Physical_characteristics)). What is settled is the reputation: the bandit look says "up to something."

I'm up to something too, just a harmless something. A silly hat, an $8 TEMU crazy shirt, and a [Tesla named Tony](/tesla) that makes fart sounds every time I get a text. The mascot wears mismatched Crocs for a reason.

## A city generalist

Raccoons thrive in cities. Kassel, Germany has 50 to 150 per square kilometer, and Germany's wild population started with two pairs of pet raccoons released in 1934 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Distribution_in_Germany)). They're also about as omnivorous as animals get: roughly 40% invertebrates, 33% plants and 27% vertebrates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Diet)). They eat whatever works where they live.

That's me as a [Mostly Car-Free Spirit](/eulogy#mostly-car-free-spirit): walking the kids to school, the subway plus a folding [bike](/bike), and bus-to-the-airport-elevator adventures that cost nothing. And it's me as a generalist: magic, balloons, juggling, kettlebells, bikes, code, writing. No single specialty, a lot of roles.

## Where the raccoon falls short

Being honest about the fit means admitting the flaw that matches. Raccoons are opportunists that raid predictable human food like trash ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Urban_raccoons)). My version is short-form video: when real work feels expensive, I reach for the cheap calories of a [TikTok thought escape](/addiction#tiktok-thought-escape). Fair cop.

The rest of the raccoon's resume, I'll pass on:

- **Nocturnal.** I'm up at 5am. A raccoon who keeps lark hours.
- **Nuisance.** I'd rather leave strangers with a smile than a mess.
- **Absent dad.** Raccoons aren't actually loners (related females share ranges, and males form loose groups of up to four), but the fathers take no part in raising the young ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon#Social_behavior)). That's the opposite of the [Family Man](/eulogy#family-man) I'm trying to be.

That last gap is the big one, and it's why I need a second animal.

## Aspirations from the crow

The crow doesn't make a cute mascot. But in every place the raccoon fails me, especially family and community, the crow is what I aspire to. Bonus: some of the best crow research is from the University of Washington, here in Seattle.

- **Family helpers.** Young American crows stay home one to six years to help raise their siblings, and in one New York study, 80% of nests had helpers ([Cornell](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/crows/hatchrep.html)). When Zach lets a scared Amelia sleep in his bed, that's a crow family. I want to give that kind of [complete attention](/eulogy#family-man).
- **Partners for the long haul.** American crow breeding pairs hold permanent territories ([Cornell](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/crows/ioc1998.htm)). That's [Tori](/eulogy#husband-to-tori---his-life-long-partner), and I'll keep it Tori-light, as promised.
- **Never forgets a face.** UW researchers trapped crows while wearing a caveman mask. Afterward, crows scolded and dive-bombed anyone wearing that mask, including crows that had never been caught, who learned the face from their peers ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2019/09/13/760666490/crows-are-they-scary-or-just-scary-smart)). I aspire to remember people that well, and the [Relationship Coach](/mortality-software#relationship-coach) in my mortality software exists because I don't do it naturally.
- **A flock, not a lone forager.** Every evening from late summer to early spring, tens of thousands of crows used to stream into a roost at UW Bothell, until the flock moved to Redmond in 2023 ([UW Bothell](https://www.uwb.edu/about/crows)). My flock is smaller. Ammon has been my weight-pledge partner for over 30 years ([Fit Fellow](/eulogy#fit-fellow)).
- **Gathers for its dead.** Crows gather around a dead crow, mostly to learn what killed it ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/151003-animals-science-crows-birds-culture-brains)). Learning from death to live better is the whole point of writing my own eulogy and building [mortality software](/mortality-software).
- **Tool maker.** Wild New Caledonian crows make hooked tools, and the hooks get them food faster ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonian_crow); [Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41559-017-0429-7)). The raccoon picks locks. The crow builds the pick. I want to be the builder: dotfiles, vim plugins, and [Larry](/larry), my AI life coach.
- **Talks, and means it.** American crows signal how dangerous a predator is through the timing of their caws ([The Condor, 2009](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/The-Condor/volume-111/issue-1/cond.2009.080057/The-Effect-of-Predator-Type-and-Danger-Level-on-the/10.1525/cond.2009.080057.full)), and carrion crows plan how many calls they'll make ([Science, 2024](https://www.technologynetworks.com/applied-sciences/news/crows-count-their-calls-387111)). Teaching out loud, through my [7 Habits video series](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_1J7GM3GLzw&list=PLJveOxX-mxxCl4YDfHMyNzMmWUMFxgC1n) and this blog, is my version.

## A raccoon who aspires to be a crow

So the raccoon stays. He's me: hands first, curious, a little mischievous, at home in the city, good at a lot of things and great at none, and sometimes digging through the trash for an easy snack. The crow is who I'm trying to grow into: the one who stays for the family, remembers the faces, shows up for the flock and learns from death.

Put that on a [eulogy](/eulogy): a raccoon who aspired to crow virtues.
