If you've read this blog, you've met him: a raccoon in rainbow glasses and mismatched Crocs, standing in for me in every role of my [eulogy](/eulogy). I picked him on a whim during a [time-off in 2022](/raccoon-history), and he stuck. The more I learn about raccoons, the more the whim looks right. He fits me, flaws included, and where he falls short, I borrow from a smarter bird: the crow.

## Hands that see

A raccoon sees the world with its hands. So do I. The things I love most are hand things: cards that change color on command, coins passing through a spectator's hand, [balloons](/balloon), and back in my 30s, juggling fire. My [Dealer of Smiles and Wonder](/eulogy#dealer-of-smiles-and-wonder) role is basically a raccoon with a deck of cards.

## The lock-picker

Give a raccoon a latch and it will fiddle until it opens, then remember how next time. That's my [Technologist](/eulogy#technologist) role: vim, tmux, a dotfile collection, a vim plugin for OneNote, and more [pet projects](/pet-projects) than I'll admit to. Puzzles that would annoy other people are the fun part for me.

Raccoons get there by trial and error: try, fail, try again. That's the shape of my discipline too. I start strong, fall off the wagon, and [restart the next day](/affirmations#do-it-anyways-deliberate-disciplined-daily) without beating myself up.

And sometimes a raccoon skips the puzzle and tips the whole thing over. I relate. My [AI journal](/ai-journal#ai-helps-me-think-by-showing-me-options) is mostly me trying everything, having AI show me a field of options, and picking by eye.

## Wet paws first

Raccoons don't wash their food. They wet their paws, which makes their touch sharper, before they handle anything.

I do the same thing every morning: 5am, a [gratitude journal](/process-journal), then 20 minutes of meditation. It's not cleaning up the day. It's getting my senses ready to handle it. That's my [Emotionally Healthy Human](/eulogy#emotionally-healthy-human) role, and it's my favorite raccoon trait.

## The mask and the mischief

The bandit mask says "up to something." I'm up to something too, just a harmless something: a silly hat, an $8 TEMU crazy shirt, and a [Tesla named Tony](/tesla) that makes fart sounds every time I get a text. The mascot wears mismatched Crocs for a reason.

## A city generalist

Raccoons love the city, and they'll eat whatever works where they live. That's me as a [Mostly Car-Free Spirit](/eulogy#mostly-car-free-spirit): walking the kids to school, the subway plus a folding [bike](/bike), and bus-to-the-airport-elevator adventures that cost nothing. And it's me as a generalist: magic, balloons, juggling, kettlebells, bikes, code, writing. No single specialty, a lot of roles.

## Where the raccoon falls short

Being honest about the fit means owning the flaw that matches. Raccoons raid the trash for easy calories. My version is short-form video: when real work feels expensive, I reach for a [TikTok thought escape](/addiction#tiktok-thought-escape). Fair cop.

The rest of the raccoon's resume, I'll pass on:

- **Up all night.** I'm up at 5am. A raccoon who keeps lark hours.
- **Nuisance.** I'd rather leave strangers with a smile than a mess.
- **Absent dad.** Raccoon fathers take no part in raising their kits. That's the opposite of the [Family Man](/eulogy#family-man) I'm trying to be.

That last gap is the big one, and it's why I need a second animal.

## Aspirations from the crow

The crow doesn't make a cute mascot. But everywhere the raccoon fails me, especially family and community, the crow is what I aspire to.

- **Family helpers.** Young crows stay home for years to help raise their little siblings. When Zach lets a scared Amelia sleep in his bed, that's a crow family. I want to give that kind of [complete attention](/eulogy#family-man).
- **Partners for the long haul.** Crow couples keep the same home together year after year. That's [Tori](/eulogy#husband-to-tori---his-life-long-partner), and I'll keep it Tori-light, as promised.
- **Never forgets a face.** Wrong a crow and its whole neighborhood will know your face. I want to remember people that well, and the [Relationship Coach](/mortality-software#relationship-coach) in my mortality software exists because I don't do it naturally.
- **A flock, not a lone forager.** Crows gather by the thousands to roost together at night. My flock is smaller: Ammon has been my weight-pledge partner for over 30 years ([Fit Fellow](/eulogy#fit-fellow)).
- **Gathers for its dead.** Crows gather around a dead crow to learn what killed it. Learning from death to live better is the whole point of writing my own eulogy and building [mortality software](/mortality-software).
- **Tool maker.** The raccoon picks the lock. The crow builds the pick. I want to be the builder: dotfiles, vim plugins, and [Larry](/larry), my AI life coach.
- **Talks, and means it.** A crow's caws tell the flock how much danger it's in. Teaching out loud, through my [7 Habits video series](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_1J7GM3GLzw&list=PLJveOxX-mxxCl4YDfHMyNzMmWUMFxgC1n) and this blog, is my version.

## A raccoon who aspires to be a crow

So the raccoon stays. He's me: hands first, curious, a little mischievous, at home in the city, good at a lot of things and great at none, and sometimes digging through the trash for an easy snack. The crow is who I'm trying to grow into: the one who stays for the family, remembers the faces, shows up for the flock and learns from death.

Put that on my [eulogy](/eulogy): a raccoon who aspired to crow virtues.
