A Facebook infographic crossed my feed: "12 Japanese Wisdoms — Ideas for a more meaningful life." My first reaction was "are these words even real?" (yes, mostly; see the [footnote](#are-these-words-real)). My second was that I already have a blog post for most of them. So here they are as raccoons, sorted into what I practice, what I'm reaching for and what's least for me.

## What I practice

### 1. Ikigai: a reason to get up

![At dawn the raccoon leaps head-first into a trash can glowing gold like a treasure chest, a Croc flying off, while the alarm clock that woke him rings on the cobbles.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/ikigai.webp)

_Mine: [begin with the end in mind](/end-in-mind), which is why I wrote my [eulogy](/eulogy)._

### 2. Kaizen: small steps, every day

![The raccoon stands on a staircase of three books, holding up a fourth to add today, climbing toward a cookie jar on top of the cupboard.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/kaizen.webp)

_Mine: [deliberate, disciplined, daily](/affirmations#do-it-anyways-deliberate-disciplined-daily)._

### 3. Shoshin: beginner's mind

![The raccoon in an orange helmet rides a tricycle with training wheels, tongue out in concentration, a green-and-yellow beginner's chevron on the back.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/shoshin.webp)

_Mine: [isn't that curious?](/curious)_

### 4. Oubaitori: bloom at your own pace

![The raccoon wears horse blinkers while he waters his tiny sprout, refusing to look at the smug owl beside him showing off an enormous sunflower.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/oubaitori.webp)

_Mine: [dealer of smiles and wonder](/eulogy#dealer-of-smiles-and-wonder)._

### 5. Gaman: endure with dignity

![In pouring rain at night the raccoon sits upright and calm on a bench, holding an umbrella blown inside out, a kitten curled up dry beside his tail.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/gaman.webp)

_Mine: [calm like water](/affirmations#calm-like-water-be-present-this-too-shall-pass-work-the-problem)._

### 6. Wabi-sabi: beauty in imperfection

![In a pottery corner the raccoon proudly holds a lumpy, lopsided mug he made himself, while a perfect white shop mug sits ignored on the shelf.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/wabi-sabi.webp)

_Mine: I enjoy amateur everything; it's just more interesting. Hence the [mismatched shoes and crazy shirts](/joy#mismatched-shoes-and-crazy-shirts-from-temu)._

## What I'm reaching for

### 7. Ichigo ichie: one time, one meeting

_Mine: [be present](/affirmations#calm-like-water-be-present-this-too-shall-pass-work-the-problem)._

### 8. Mono no aware: the beauty of passing things

![The felt raccoon in rainbow glasses on a bench under falling cherry blossoms at sunset, savoring an ice-cream cone that is melting down his paw.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/mono-no-aware.webp)

_Mine: [life is finite, time is limited](/mortality-software)._

### 9. Hara hachi bu: eat until 80% full

![At an izakaya counter, eight dumpling spots on the plate are empty; with a single heroic tear the raccoon pushes away the last two.](/images/japanese-wisdoms/hara-hachi-bu.webp)

_Mine: [no mindless eating](/diet#a-tale-of-mindless-eating)._

## Are these words real?

Yes, all 12 are genuine Japanese. The infographic glosses drift, though: a real word picks up a self-help meaning on its way to English. The [ikigai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikigai) four-circle Venn diagram isn't Japanese ([its history](https://ikigaitribe.com/ikigai/podcast05/)). [Kaizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaizen) is an everyday word for "improvement" that a 1986 management book turned into a doctrine. [Shinrin-yoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinrin-yoku) was coined by Japan's forestry ministry in 1982. In Buddhist usage [gaman](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaman_(term)>) meant arrogance ([我慢, Kotobank](https://kotobank.jp/word/我慢)), and [oubaitori](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桜梅桃李) (桜梅桃李) is an obscure idiom whose "stop comparing" meaning is a modern spin. The rest: [mono no aware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_no_aware), [wabi-sabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabi-sabi) (侘び寂び), [shoshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshin), [ichigo ichie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichi-go_ichi-e), [mottainai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mottainai), [hara hachi bu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hara_hachi_bun_me), [kintsugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintsugi). A word can be useful even when the meme about it is wrong. That's [The Words We Don't Have](/words-we-dont-have).
