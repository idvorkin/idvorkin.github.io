{% comment %} Time-allocation buckets — five bucket rows for a 14 hour day to spend (24h minus 8 sleep minus about 2 for meals and transit), drawn as one day with arrows between the buckets. Controls: a Weekday / Weekend flip (two made-up baselines), a No plan / Planned switch (Planned is the target allocation), two sliders for work and my own tech, and a "push the hour up / let it slide back" stepper that walks one hour up the ladder consumption -> my own tech -> work. Sliding below a baseline frees hours that flow by the plan's shares with ceilings (relationships "their time", health "enough"; anything past a ceiling runs into consumption; with no plan most of it goes straight to consumption). Sliding above a baseline takes hours from consumption first, then identity, health and relationships. The day always adds up to 14 hours and a stacked day strip shows the total. The Tech bar is split into work and my own. The arrows in the left gutter are an SVG drawn from the computed flows every render; the ceiling notes, the "counts for" readouts, the day strip and the summary line update in the same pass. Self-contained, no parameters, no libraries, no network. Edit the DATA block at the top of the script. THE HOURS ARE ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT MEASURED: plausible round numbers, not a log of anyone's week. Move an hour

Fourteen hours to spend, made up to show the shape: 24, less 8 asleep, less about 2 for meals and transit. Pick a weekday or a weekend, set work and my own tech, and watch where the hours go; Planned is my target. Then push one hour up the screen, from the phone into my own tech into work, or let it slide back.

Point the hour
    The whole day

    24h, less 8 asleep, less about 2 for meals and transit, leaves 14 to spend. Moving a slider never changes the total: hours come from, or go to, the other buckets.

    The arrows show where the last move went. The dashed tick is a ceiling: Relationships stops at whatever time the other people actually have, Health stops at enough. Hours taken out of tech land by the plan; planned hours that run past a ceiling do not disappear, they land in consumption, and hours with no plan mostly go there directly. Hours added to tech come out of the phone first, then identity, health and relationships. Tech is two segments, work and my own, because the same screen hour counts for very different amounts depending on where it points. The weekday and weekend baselines, and how much an hour counts for, are made up, not measured.
