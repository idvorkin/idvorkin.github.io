{% comment %} Time-allocation buckets — five bucket rows for a 16 hour waking day, a "hours out of tech" slider, and a No plan / Planned switch. Planned hours go to relationships, identity and health until relationships hits "their time" and health hits "enough"; anything past a ceiling runs into consumption. With no plan, most of the freed hours go straight to consumption. Self-contained, no parameters, no libraries, no network. Edit the DATA block at the top of the script; the rows, the ceiling ticks and the leak line are all generated from it. Below the rows, a static "which way the hour moves" strip splits the Tech bucket into my own tech and work, and shows the direction energy flows (consumption → tech → work) and what an hour counts for. Plain HTML/CSS, no script. THE HOURS ARE ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT MEASURED: plausible round numbers for a 16 hour waking day, not a log of anyone's week. Used by: _d/time-allocation.md {% endcomment %}

Take hours out of tech

Sixteen waking hours, made up to show the shape. Move the slider, then switch between an hour with no plan and an hour with one. Planned is my target.

    Which way the hour moves

    Hitting the target is hard. Changing direction isn't. Here the Tech bucket splits in two, my own tech and work, and the same screen hour counts for very different amounts depending on where it points.

    Consumption The scroll. Same screen, nothing made. counts for ~0
    upgrade
    Tech, my own Building instead of scrolling. counts for 1 hour
    best case
    Tech, at work Aligned, with a company behind me. counts for far more

    It slides back just as easily, one tired tab at a time.

    The dashed tick is a ceiling. Relationships stops at whatever time the other people actually have. Health stops at enough. Planned hours that run past a ceiling do not disappear, they land in consumption. Hours with no plan mostly go there directly. Numbers, and how much an hour counts for, are made up, not measured.
